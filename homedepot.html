Business case: DIY Project & Material Planner

Prepared by Rahul Dara for Pricing & Merchandising, The Home Depot Canada. Concept stage; all prices and margins are sample assumptions.

The problem

  • DIY customers miss adding smaller products to their basket and need to make 2 to 3 trips per project.
  • Home Depot misses out on AOV, and on the margin gained from cross-bundling and cross-selling.

The product

  • Turns project dimensions into a complete, code-aware material list in build order.
  • Priced at the customer's store, with provincial tax applied.
  • Good, Better and Best for the whole project, then adjustable line by line.
  • Owned items removed in one click; low-use tools offered as rentals.
  • The full list goes to the cart, or prints as a store list.

Success measures

  • Average order value and units per basket
  • Conversion by customer segment and cohort
  • Attach rate on basket-builder lines
  • Better and Best grade mix
  • Blended project margin
  • Plan-to-cart conversion; return trips per project

Pricing and merchandising levers

  • Perception drivers (price image): Lumber, concrete, deck screws and the entry drill kit stay sharply priced and visible per unit, so the prices customers check are the prices that win the comparison.
  • Basket builders (margin on small tickets): Hanger nails, staples, safety gear, end-cut preservative and tape are added automatically: low ticket, high attach, rarely price-checked.
  • Cross-sell (attach rate): Build-phase logic surfaces the categories DIYers forget, such as flashing tape, post caps and landscape fabric.
  • Cross-bundling (basket completion): A 5% project bundle saving on basket builders, funded from their own margin, rewards buying the full list in one place.
  • Upsell (grade mix): Good, Better and Best at two levels: a starting grade for the whole project, then a per-line choice. Customers can put Best where they see and touch it (decking, finish, tools) and Good where it is hidden (framing).
  • Segments and cohorts (conversion): Every plan reveals project, scope, tool ownership, rentals and where the customer chose to trade up. Those signals place the customer in a segment and a cohort, so each is shown the right selection and the right grade first.
  • Planned second visit (retention): New pressure-treated lumber must dry about 60 days before staining, so finish is scheduled as a second purchase with a dated reminder rather than sold on day one.
  • Low-margin additions (lifetime value): Safety gear and site prep lower basket margin slightly, but they protect project success and the next visit.
  • Volume pricing (larger baskets): Existing bulk tiers (buy 72 or more, save 10%) apply automatically when a plan qualifies.
  • Seasonal (inventory and promo): Deck and fence plans peak in spring and summer, paint in fall and winter. Aggregated plans inform flyer timing, allocation and markdowns.
  • Tool rental (service revenue): Rent or buy on low-use tools drives Tool Rental counter traffic at rental margins.

Business requirements

  • Each list line mapped to a store SKU at each grade
  • Store-level price, stock and bulk tiers
  • Grade ladders owned by Merchandising
  • Guardrail: the planner never changes a KVI price; promotions always honoured
  • Bundle funding rules set by Pricing

Technical requirements

  • Rules-based quantity engine with unit tests
  • Catalogue, price and inventory APIs per store
  • Add full list to cart
  • Tax for all 10 provinces; bilingual EN/FR
  • Analytics events and A/B testing; WCAG 2.1 AA
  • Consent-based customer profile on the account (PIPEDA); aggregate cohorts by default

Risks and mitigations

  • Total looks expensive: show the Good total and per-unit KVI prices first
  • Estimate errors: code-cited rules and plain disclaimers
  • Bundle dilution: limited to basket builders, capped at 5%
  • Profiling feels intrusive: opt-in only, and the customer sees why each suggestion appears

Phasing

  • Phase 1 (this page): business case and working planner with internal pricing tags.
  • Phase 2: customer-facing view, live store pricing and stock, add to cart.
  • Phase 3: pilot in two provinces to test the segment hypothesis, then amend the rules from the results.
  • Phase 4: scale cohort-based targeting nationally.
